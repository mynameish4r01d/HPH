Inside a solar cell

Each panel is made of solar cells: thin layers of silicon. When light (tiny packets of energy called photons) hits a cell, it knocks electrons loose. The cell's layers push those electrons one way, so they flow out through a wire as electricity. This is called the photovoltaic effect.

Shade means fewer photons reach the cells, so fewer electrons flow. The bulb follows the total output of the panels above.

Why shading matters

  1. 1 Shade cuts a panel's output Less light on the cells means less electricity. Even a branch or a patch of dirt makes a difference.
  2. 2 String inverters share the problem Panels wired in one string all carry the same current, so one shaded panel holds the others back, or gets skipped entirely.
  3. 3 Micro inverters keep it contained The MX2250 gives every panel its own input, so each one makes as much as its own light allows. Shade only costs the shaded panel.
  4. 4 More power over the year Shade moves through the day. Keeping every unshaded panel at full power adds up to more energy, and a lower bill.
